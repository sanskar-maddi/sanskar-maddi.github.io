The question

How does a liquid interface respond to the sudden arrival of a shock wave?

Project overview

My doctoral research examines the rapid response of droplets and liquid jets to shock-wave loading. High-speed observations connect the evolution of a liquid interface with the flow and pressure history that drives it.

This work sits at the intersection of compressible flow, interfacial dynamics, and experimental diagnostics. The aim is to understand the progression from initial deformation to breakup.

Approach & methods

01

Observe the interface

High-speed backlit imaging captures changes in liquid shape over time.

02

Resolve the surrounding flow

Schlieren diagnostics reveal density-gradient structures associated with the high-speed flow.

03

Connect loading and response

Pressure measurements and image analysis relate the loading history to deformation and breakup.

Areas of focus

  • Shock-driven droplet deformation and breakup
  • Liquid-jet response to transient loading
  • Flow-regime mapping and experimental diagnostics
