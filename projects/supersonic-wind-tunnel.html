The question

How can a vacuum-driven facility create an accessible environment for supersonic-flow experiments?

Project overview

My master’s thesis at IIT Bombay focused on developing an in-draft, vacuum-driven supersonic wind tunnel. An axisymmetric de Laval nozzle and a vacuum chamber form the basis of a facility designed to generate short-duration, approximately Mach 2 flow.

The project brings together aerodynamic design, computational analysis, mechanical integration, and experimental diagnostics. Optical access and pressure measurements are central to making the flow useful for research.

Approach & methods

01

Design the nozzle

Develop an axisymmetric de Laval nozzle using compressible-flow design methods.

02

Analyze the facility

Use computational fluid dynamics to study the nozzle and the vacuum-driven flow configuration.

03

Plan the measurements

Integrate pressure diagnostics and optical access for observing the test-section flow.

Areas of focus

  • Axisymmetric de Laval nozzle design
  • Vacuum-driven, short-duration supersonic flow
  • Facility integration and diagnostic access
